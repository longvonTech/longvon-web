## Draft

Choosing a smart ring OEM/ODM partner is not the same as choosing a consumer brand. Procurement teams need a manufacturer that can discuss hardware, firmware, health algorithms, companion apps, partner SDK/API access, and manufacturing controls under a clear project scope.

### 1. Confirm who the legal manufacturer is
Ask for the legal company name and whether the consumer brand on the box is the same entity. LONGVON Technology (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd. is the technology manufacturer and OEM/ODM provider; MATEYOU is its smart ring product brand — not the manufacturer.

### 2. Map cooperation model to your maturity
- **Existing Product OEM** — brand/packaging adjustments on an agreed platform
- **Small Batch / Pilot** — evaluation and pilot builds before volume (quantities confirmed after RFQ; avoid partners that force you into invented public MOQ if your risk profile differs)
- **Customized ODM** — ID/hardware/firmware/algorithm/App/SDK scope
- **Full Technology Solution** — requirements through stack + manufacturing support

### 3. Separate SDK, algorithms, support, and cloud
Require written boundaries: what data the SDK exposes (including whether 100Hz PPG raw data is in scope), which algorithms need cloud services, and what is licensed separately. Do not accept vague promises of “all algorithms offline” or “unrestricted Bluetooth protocol” unless the statement of work proves it.

### 4. Health claims and regulatory honesty
For consumer reference products such as Ring1C, treat them as health-monitoring electronics — not medical devices — unless the partner provides verified registration facts for a specific SKU and market. Chinese regulatory progress is not overseas medical-device approval.

### 5. Evidence checklist
Legal identity, R&D/manufacturing narrative, quality language (prefer “ISO 13485–oriented” unless a certificate is on file), sample/evaluation path, and an English RFQ channel for international buyers.
